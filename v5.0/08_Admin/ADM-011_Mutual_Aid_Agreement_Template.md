## 1. Purpose

This document provides a template for mutual aid agreements between Aeria Solutions Ltd and other RPAS operators in British Columbia. Mutual aid agreements enable operators to support each other during emergencies, equipment failures, or capacity constraints while maintaining safety and regulatory compliance.

---

## 2. Scope

This template applies to:
- Mutual aid arrangements with other BC RPAS operators
- Emergency resource sharing
- Capacity support during peak demand
- Equipment and personnel sharing
- Knowledge and expertise sharing

---

## 3. Benefits of Mutual Aid

| Benefit | Description |
|:--------|:------------|
| Business continuity | Support during equipment failures or key person absence |
| Capacity management | Handle demand spikes without permanent expansion |
| Emergency response | Rapid response capability for urgent situations |
| Cost efficiency | Share specialized equipment or expertise |
| Industry collaboration | Strengthen professional relationships |
| Client service | Maintain service levels during challenges |

---

## 4. Scope of Mutual Aid

### 4.1 Resources Subject to Mutual Aid

| Resource Category | Examples |
|:------------------|:---------|
| Personnel | Pilots, observers, ground crew |
| RPAS aircraft | Platforms, sensors, payloads |
| Ground equipment | Vehicles, charging equipment, tools |
| Facilities | Office space, storage, staging areas |
| Expertise | Specialized knowledge, training support |
| Data processing | Software, processing capacity |

### 4.2 Types of Assistance

| Type | Description | Typical Duration |
|:-----|:------------|:-----------------|
| Emergency | Immediate response to crisis | Hours to days |
| Scheduled | Planned support for known shortage | Days to weeks |
| Expertise | Technical consultation or training | As needed |
| Equipment only | Loan of equipment without personnel | Variable |

### 4.3 Operational Limitations

Mutual aid does not include:
- Operations outside either party's certificate scope
- Activities requiring additional regulatory approval not held
- Operations either party deems unsafe
- Conflicts with existing client commitments
- Any arrangement violating regulations

## 5. Activation Procedures

### 5.1 Activation Levels

| Level | Situation | Response Time |
|:------|:----------|:--------------|
| Level 1 - Emergency | Life safety, regulatory deadline, critical failure | 2-4 hours |
| Level 2 - Urgent | Equipment down, personnel unavailable | 24-48 hours |
| Level 3 - Planned | Known future need, capacity support | 1+ weeks notice |

### 5.2 Activation Process

**Level 1 - Emergency Activation:**
1. Contact partner operator primary contact (phone)
2. Describe situation and resources needed
3. Confirm availability and response time
4. Verbal agreement to activate
5. Written confirmation within 24 hours
6. Deploy resources

**Level 2 - Urgent Activation:**
1. Contact partner operator (phone or email)
2. Describe need and timeline
3. Discuss available resources
4. Confirm terms and logistics
5. Written confirmation before deployment
6. Deploy resources

**Level 3 - Planned Activation:**
1. Contact partner operator (email)
2. Provide detailed scope and timeline
3. Negotiate terms and logistics
4. Execute written agreement addendum
5. Coordinate deployment
6. Deploy resources

### 5.3 Decision Authority

| Decision | Authority |
|:---------|:----------|
| Request mutual aid | Accountable Executive or Operations Manager |
| Accept request | Accountable Executive |
| Deploy personnel | Operations Manager |
| Deploy equipment | Operations Manager |
| Terminate activation | Accountable Executive (either party) |

### 5.4 Confirmation Requirements

All activations require written confirmation including:
- Resources provided
- Duration of assistance
- Cost recovery terms
- Operational command structure
- Liability acknowledgment
- Emergency contacts

## 7. Liability and Insurance

### 7.1 Insurance Requirements

Both parties must maintain:

| Coverage | Minimum |
|:---------|:--------|
| Aviation liability | $2,000,000 |
| Commercial general liability | $2,000,000 |
| WorkSafeBC or equivalent | Per BC requirements |

### 7.2 Liability Allocation

| Situation | Liability |
|:----------|:----------|
| Personnel injury - providing operator's personnel | Providing operator's WorkSafeBC |
| Equipment damage - normal operations | Receiving operator |
| Equipment damage - providing operator negligence | Providing operator |
| Third-party claims | Per operational command (receiving operator typically) |
| Regulatory violations | Operator who committed violation |

### 7.3 Indemnification

Each party agrees to indemnify the other for:
- Claims arising from their own negligence
- Claims arising from their personnel's actions
- Claims arising from their equipment failures
- Regulatory penalties resulting from their violations

### 7.4 Insurance Verification

- Each party provides current Certificate of Insurance
- Certificates updated upon renewal
- Coverage verified before each activation

## 10. Agreement Administration

### 10.1 Agreement Term

| Item | Standard |
|:-----|:---------|
| Initial term | One year from effective date |
| Renewal | Automatic annual renewal |
| Termination | 30 days written notice |
| Immediate termination | Material breach, safety concerns |

### 10.2 Agreement Review

- Annual review of terms and rates
- Update contact information as needed
- Review and update insurance coverage
- Address any issues from activations

### 10.3 Amendments

- Amendments in writing signed by both parties
- Email confirmation acceptable for minor changes
- Rate changes require 30 days notice

### 10.4 Confidentiality

Both parties agree to:
- Protect confidential business information
- Not disclose client information
- Maintain confidentiality of agreement terms
- Exceptions: regulatory requirements, legal process
