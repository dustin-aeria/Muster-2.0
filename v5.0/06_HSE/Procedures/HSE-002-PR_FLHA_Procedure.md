## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how to conduct Field Level Hazard Assessments (FLHAs) before and during work to identify site-specific and day-specific hazards not covered by Formal Hazard Assessments.

---

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:
- All field operations
- All worksites (including client sites)
- All personnel participating in field work

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 When to Complete an FLHA

Complete an FLHA:
- Before starting work at any site
- At the start of each work day
- When conditions change significantly
- When new hazards are identified
- When moving to a different work area

### 4.2 FLHA Process

#### Step 1: Stop and Observe
Before starting work:
- Walk the site perimeter
- Look for hazards in all directions (up, down, around)
- Consider weather and environmental conditions
- Check for changes since last assessment

#### Step 2: Gather the Crew
- All crew members participate in the FLHA
- Each person contributes observations
- Everyone signs the completed FLHA

#### Step 3: Identify Hazards

Consider these categories:

| Category | Examples |
|:---------|:---------|
| **Physical** | Terrain, obstacles, traffic, weather, noise |
| **Environmental** | Wildlife, insects, water, contamination |
| **Equipment** | RPAS, vehicles, tools, power sources |
| **Task-Related** | Workload, complexity, time pressure |
| **Human Factors** | Fatigue, fitness, training, communication |
| **Site-Specific** | Client rules, restricted areas, other workers |

Use the FLHA form prompts to ensure nothing is missed.

#### Step 4: Assess Risk

For each significant hazard:
- Assess likelihood (high/medium/low)
- Assess severity (high/medium/low)
- Determine risk level using matrix

| Risk Level | Action Required |
|:-----------|:----------------|
| **Critical** | Do not proceed until risk is reduced |
| **High** | Contact Operations Manager; implement additional controls |
| **Medium** | Implement controls; proceed with caution |
| **Low** | Awareness; standard procedures |

#### Step 5: Identify Controls

For each hazard, determine:
- What controls will reduce the risk
- Who is responsible for implementing
- How effectiveness will be verified

Controls should follow the hierarchy:
1. Can we eliminate the hazard?
2. Can we substitute something safer?
3. Can we physically separate workers from the hazard?
4. What procedures or warnings do we need?
5. What PPE is required?

#### Step 6: Document and Brief

- Record all findings on FRM-FLHA
- Brief all crew members
- Ensure everyone understands hazards and controls
- Have all participants sign the FLHA

#### Step 7: Keep FLHA Active

The FLHA is a living document:
- Update if conditions change
- Re-brief if new hazards appear
- Add notes as work progresses
- Complete weather updates if required

### 4.3 FLHA Form Completion

The FLHA form includes:

| Section | Content |
|:--------|:--------|
| **Header** | Date, location, project, crew names |
| **Weather** | Current and forecast conditions |
| **Site Info** | Site description, access, emergency contacts |
| **Hazard Checklist** | Standard hazard prompts with checkboxes |
| **Hazard Details** | Specific hazards identified |
| **Controls** | Control measures for each hazard |
| **Emergency Info** | Hospital, EMS, muster point |
| **Signatures** | All participants |

### 4.4 What Makes a Good FLHA

**Good FLHAs:**
- Are site-specific, not generic
- Involve all crew members
- Identify actual conditions
- Have clear, actionable controls
- Are updated when conditions change

**Poor FLHAs:**
- Copy-paste from previous days
- Single person completes alone
- Generic hazards only
- Vague controls ("be careful")
- Never updated

### 4.5 Continuous Monitoring

During work:
- Stay alert for changing conditions
- Watch for new hazards
- Communicate concerns immediately
- Stop work if new risks emerge
- Update FLHA as needed
