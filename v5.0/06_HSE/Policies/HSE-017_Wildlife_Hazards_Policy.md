## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for protecting workers from wildlife hazards during field operations in British Columbia and other operating areas.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All Aeria Solutions field operations
- All personnel working in areas with wildlife hazards
- Operations in wilderness, rural, and industrial sites where wildlife may be present

## 4. Policy

### 4.1 Wildlife Hazard Recognition

Workers shall be aware of wildlife hazards including:

| Species | Hazard | BC Prevalence |
|:--------|:-------|:--------------|
| **Black Bear** | Attacks when surprised, protecting cubs, or food-conditioned | Common throughout BC |
| **Grizzly Bear** | Aggressive if surprised; more dangerous than black bear | Interior, northern, coastal BC |
| **Cougar** | Stalking predator; rare but serious attacks | Throughout BC |
| **Moose** | Aggressive when surprised; vehicle collisions | Throughout BC |
| **Wolves/Coyotes** | Generally avoid humans; occasional aggression | Throughout BC |
| **Bees/Wasps** | Anaphylactic reactions; multiple stings | Common |
| **Ticks** | Lyme disease and other illnesses | Southern and coastal BC |
| **Venomous Snakes** | Rattlesnake bites (interior BC) | Southern interior BC |

### 4.2 Pre-Work Assessment

Before field operations, PICs shall assess:
- Known wildlife activity in the area
- Recent sightings or encounters reported
- Seasonal considerations (bears emerging in spring, rutting season, etc.)
- Site-specific client information
- Appropriate controls and equipment

Document wildlife considerations on FLHA.

### 4.3 Bear Safety

#### Prevention:
- Make noise while moving through brush
- Travel in groups when possible
- Store food in bear-resistant containers or vehicles
- Do not leave food or garbage unattended
- Be extra vigilant near streams, berry patches, and dense cover
- Carry bear spray and know how to use it

#### If You Encounter a Bear:
1. Stay calm; do not run
2. Speak calmly; identify yourself as human
3. Back away slowly if the bear has not seen you
4. If bear approaches: stand your ground, appear large
5. Use bear spray if bear charges (within 6 meters)
6. If attacked by black bear: fight back
7. If attacked by grizzly: protect vital areas; play dead if defensive attack

### 4.4 Cougar Safety

#### Prevention:
- Travel in groups
- Make noise
- Keep children and small individuals close
- Do not approach cougars

#### If You Encounter a Cougar:
1. Do not run; do not crouch
2. Face the cougar; maintain eye contact
3. Appear large; speak firmly
4. Back away slowly
5. If attacked: fight back aggressively

### 4.5 Equipment Requirements

| Situation | Required Equipment |
|:----------|:------------------|
| **Bear country operations** | Bear spray (minimum 225g); air horn |
| **Remote operations** | Bear spray; first aid kit; communication device |
| **Known high-activity areas** | Bear spray; bear bangers; consider armed escort |

Bear spray shall be:
- Carried on person (not in pack)
- Readily accessible
- Within expiry date
- Understood by all crew

### 4.6 Training

Personnel working in wildlife areas shall be trained in:
- Wildlife species identification
- Encounter avoidance
- Encounter response
- Bear spray use
- First aid for wildlife injuries
- Reporting requirements

### 4.7 Reporting

Report all wildlife encounters to:
- PIC immediately
- Operations Manager same day
- Conservation Officer Service if required (attacks, aggressive wildlife)

Document encounters on FLHA or incident report.

### 4.8 RPAS and Wildlife

RPAS operations near wildlife:
- Maintain distance to avoid disturbing wildlife
- Cease operations if wildlife shows disturbance behavior
- Follow any wildlife-related SFOC conditions
- Do not pursue or harass wildlife
- Report wildlife strikes or near-misses

## 5. Seasonal Considerations

| Season | Considerations |
|:-------|:---------------|
| **Spring** | Bears emerging, hungry; with cubs |
| **Summer** | Bears active; berry season; nesting wildlife |
| **Fall** | Bears hyperphagia (pre-hibernation feeding); rutting moose/elk |
| **Winter** | Reduced bear activity; wolves more active; vehicle/wildlife collisions |
