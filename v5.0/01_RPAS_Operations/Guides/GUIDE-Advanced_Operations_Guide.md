## 1. Introduction

This guide provides information for pilots conducting Advanced Operations under CARs Part IX. Advanced Operations require a Pilot Certificate - Advanced Operations and involve operations in or near controlled airspace, near people, or in other complex situations.

---

## 2. Advanced vs. Basic Operations

### 2.1 When Advanced Certificate Required

| Scenario | Certificate Required |
|:---------|:--------------------|
| Uncontrolled airspace, away from people | Basic |
| Controlled airspace | Advanced |
| Within 30m horizontally of bystanders | Advanced |
| Within 5m vertically of bystanders | Advanced |
| Over bystanders (specific conditions) | Advanced |
| Near aerodromes with authorization | Advanced |

### 2.2 Advanced Pilot Privileges

Advanced pilots may:
- Fly in controlled airspace (with authorization)
- Fly closer to bystanders (per conditions)
- Conduct more complex operations
- Act as PIC for BVLOS (with additional authorization)

---

## 3. Controlled Airspace Operations

### 3.1 Airspace Classes

| Class | Typical Location | RPAS Requirements |
|:------|:-----------------|:------------------|
| **A** | High level | Not applicable to RPAS |
| **B** | Major airport terminal | Authorization required |
| **C** | Medium airport terminal | Authorization required |
| **D** | Smaller controlled airports | Authorization required |
| **E** | Low-level controlled | Varies |

### 3.2 Authorization Process

**NAV CANADA System:**
1. Log in to NAV CANADA RPAS portal
2. Create flight request
3. Specify location, date/time, altitude
4. Include RPOC number
5. Submit and await authorization
6. Do not operate until authorized

**Lead Time:**
- Minimum 24-48 hours
- Complex operations: longer
- Last-minute requests may not be approved

### 3.3 Operating in Controlled Airspace

When authorized:
- Operate only within authorized parameters
- Monitor ATC frequencies if required
- Be aware of manned aircraft traffic
- Be prepared to land immediately if directed
- Report any incidents to ATC

---

## 4. Operations Near People

### 4.1 Regulatory Distances

| Situation | Minimum Distance |
|:----------|:-----------------|
| Bystanders (horizontal) - Basic | >30m |
| Bystanders (horizontal) - Advanced | May fly closer per conditions |
| Bystanders (vertical) - Basic | Not above |
| Bystanders (vertical) - Advanced | >5m above (conditions apply) |
| Spectators (at organized event) | Per SFOC |

### 4.2 Conditions for Closer Operations

Advanced pilots may fly within 30m of bystanders if:
- Aircraft weight ≤250g, OR
- Operating a reviewed aircraft, AND
- Maintaining appropriate safety margins

**Reviewed Aircraft:**
Aircraft that has been reviewed by Transport Canada as safe for closer operations to people.

### 4.3 Risk Mitigation

When operating closer to people:
- Brief people on operation if possible
- Establish clear no-fly areas
- Maintain heightened awareness
- Be prepared for immediate action
- Have clear egress paths
- Consider using smaller aircraft

---

## 5. Near-Aerodrome Operations

### 5.1 Distance Requirements

| Distance from Aerodrome | Requirement |
|:------------------------|:------------|
| >5.6 km (3 NM) | No restriction for aerodrome |
| 1.9-5.6 km | May require authorization |
| <1.9 km | Authorization required |
| Within aerodrome boundary | Typically not permitted |

### 5.2 Authorization Methods

- NAV CANADA RPAS system
- Direct contact with aerodrome operator (uncontrolled)
- ATC coordination (controlled)

### 5.3 Safe Operations Near Aerodromes

- Know active runways and traffic patterns
- Monitor aviation radio if equipped
- Be prepared to land immediately
- Give way to all manned aircraft
- Be extra vigilant for traffic

---

## 6. Complex Mission Planning

### 6.1 Elements of Complex Missions

Complex operations may include:
- Multiple flight areas
- Extended duration
- Multiple aircraft
- Payload changes
- Crew rotations
- Client coordination

### 6.2 Planning Approach

1. **Define mission scope** clearly
2. **Break into segments** if complex
3. **Identify all requirements** (airspace, permissions)
4. **Plan crew assignments** and rotations
5. **Develop contingencies** for issues
6. **Brief thoroughly** before starting

### 6.3 Multi-Aircraft Operations

When multiple aircraft operating:
- Deconfliction plan required
- Clear communication between PICs
- Altitude separation or lateral separation
- One aircraft per PIC
- Coordinated launch/recovery

---

## 7. Client Site Operations

### 7.1 Pre-Operation Coordination

Before arriving at client site:
- Understand client requirements
- Complete required orientations
- Obtain permits/access
- Coordinate operation timing
- Establish communication plan

### 7.2 On-Site Operations

- Follow client safety requirements
- Coordinate with site personnel
- Report any incidents immediately
- Maintain professional conduct
- Document operations thoroughly

---

## 8. Weather Considerations for Advanced Operations

### 8.1 Enhanced Weather Assessment

For complex operations:
- More detailed weather planning
- Consider site-specific effects
- Plan alternatives for marginal weather
- Conservative go/no-go decisions

### 8.2 Urban/Industrial Effects

Near buildings and structures:
- Mechanical turbulence (wind around buildings)
- Updrafts and downdrafts
- EMI interference
- GPS reflections (multipath)
- Reduced visibility between structures

### 9.1 Pre-Operation
