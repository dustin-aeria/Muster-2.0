## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose

This guide provides comprehensive procedures for conducting RPAS operations from remote camps, including temporary field bases, exploration camps, and isolated work sites where normal infrastructure is unavailable.

### 1.2 Scope

This guide applies to:
- Operations from camps more than 2 hours from emergency services
- Sites without reliable cellular coverage
- Operations requiring helicopter or float plane access
- Multi-day remote deployments
- Operations in wilderness areas with wildlife hazards

## 2. Remote Camp Definition and Classification

### 2.1 Remote Site Classification

| Classification | Criteria | Requirements |
|:---------------|:---------|:-------------|
| **Level 1 - Near Remote** | 1-2 hours from services, cell coverage available | Standard procedures, enhanced communication |
| **Level 2 - Remote** | 2-4 hours from services, limited/no cell coverage | Satellite communication, extended supplies |
| **Level 3 - Isolated** | >4 hours from services, helicopter/boat access only | Full self-sufficiency, emergency extraction plan |

### 2.2 Minimum Crew Requirements

| Classification | Minimum Personnel | Working Alone |
|:---------------|:------------------|:--------------|
| **Level 1** | 1 (with check-ins) | Permitted with protocols |
| **Level 2** | 2 | Not permitted |
| **Level 3** | 2 (3 recommended) | Not permitted |

## 3. Camp Setup for RPAS Operations

### 3.1 Site Selection Criteria

**Operational requirements:**

| Factor | Requirement |
|:-------|:------------|
| **Launch/landing area** | Minimum 10m x 10m clear, level surface |
| **Obstacle clearance** | No obstructions within 30m above 30-degree angle |
| **Magnetic interference** | Away from large metal structures, generators |
| **Line of sight** | Clear sight lines to operating area |
| **Wind exposure** | Sheltered from prevailing winds if possible |

**Camp integration:**
- Separation from helicopter pad (minimum 100m)
- Away from fuel storage
- Accessible to power source
- Clear of high-traffic areas

### 3.2 Operations Area Layout

**Recommended layout:**

```
                    [OPERATING AREA]
                          |
                          | 50m minimum
                          |
    [HELIPD] ---- 100m ----[RPAS LAUNCH AREA]---- 30m ----[GCS/CHARGING]
                                   |
                                   | 20m
                                   |
                          [EQUIPMENT STORAGE]
                                   |
                                   | 30m
                                   |
                            [CAMP/LODGING]
```

### 3.3 Launch Area Preparation

**Surface preparation:**
- Clear debris, rocks, vegetation
- Mark boundaries clearly (flagging, cones)
- Establish wind indicator (windsock, flagging tape)
- Create emergency landing zone
- Secure loose items in area

**Markings:**
- Launch point marked with high-visibility marker
- Compass rose or north indicator
- Pilot position marked
- Observer positions if applicable

## 4. Equipment Logistics and Power

### 4.1 Equipment Transport Considerations

| Transport Method | Weight Limits | Considerations |
|:-----------------|:--------------|:---------------|
| **Helicopter** | Varies by aircraft (typically 500-1000 lbs) | Package for sling or internal load |
| **Float plane** | Typically 400-600 lbs | Waterproof cases essential |
| **ATV/UTV** | 300-500 lbs | Secure against vibration |
| **Backpack/human carry** | 30-50 lbs per person | Limit to essential equipment |

### 4.2 Equipment Packing Requirements

**All remote deployments:**
- Hard-sided, waterproof cases (Pelican or equivalent)
- Foam inserts preventing movement
- External case labeling
- Equipment inventory in each case
- Backup critical items (batteries, props, cables)

**Helicopter/aircraft transport:**
- Net-compatible packaging
- No loose items
- Total weight documented
- Hazmat declaration for batteries if required

### 4.3 Power Requirements

**Power consumption estimates:**

| Equipment | Power Requirement | Daily Usage (8hr ops) |
|:----------|:------------------|:----------------------|
| **Battery charger (per port)** | 100-200W | 4-6 hours |
| **Tablet/GCS** | 30-60W | 8 hours |
| **Communication equipment** | 20-50W | Continuous |
| **Lighting** | 50-100W | 4 hours |
| **Total estimated** | 300-500W continuous | 3-5 kWh/day |

### 4.4 Power Generation Options

| Source | Capacity | Runtime | Notes |
|:-------|:---------|:--------|:------|
| **Portable generator** | 2000-3000W | 8-12 hrs/tank | Primary source, fuel logistics |
| **Solar panels** | 100-400W | Daylight hours | Supplementary, weather dependent |
| **Battery power station** | 500-2000Wh | 2-6 hours charging | Silent operation, limited capacity |
| **Camp generator** | 5000W+ | Continuous | If available at camp |

**Fuel planning:**
- Generator: 0.5-1.0 L/hour at load
- Plan for 120% of estimated fuel need
- Fuel storage in approved containers
- Refueling away from operations area

### 4.5 Charging Station Setup

**Requirements:**
- Level, stable surface
- Weather protection (tent/tarp)
- Ventilation for battery charging
- Fire extinguisher within reach
- Away from sleeping areas
- Temperature monitoring capability

## 5. Communication Requirements

### 5.1 Communication Equipment

**Required for all remote operations:**

| Equipment | Purpose | Redundancy |
|:----------|:--------|:-----------|
| **Satellite messenger** (InReach, SPOT) | Emergency communication, tracking | Primary emergency |
| **Satellite phone** | Voice communication | Backup to messenger |
| **VHF radio** | Local communication, aircraft | Camp integration |
| **Cell phone** | Communication if coverage exists | Opportunistic use |

**Level 3 (Isolated) additional:**
- HF radio if camp-equipped
- Aviation VHF for helicopter coordination
- Second satellite device

### 5.2 Communication Protocols

**Check-in schedule:**

| Situation | Check-In Frequency | Method |
|:----------|:-------------------|:-------|
| **Normal operations** | Morning and evening | Satellite messenger |
| **During flight operations** | Every 2 hours | Satellite messenger |
| **Overdue** | After 1 hour missed check-in | Satellite phone call |
| **Emergency** | Immediate | SOS activation |

**Check-in message content:**
- Personnel status (all OK / concern)
- Location (if moved)
- Current activity
- Next check-in time
- Any issues or needs

### 5.3 Emergency Communication

**SOS activation criteria:**
- Medical emergency requiring evacuation
- Fire threatening safety
- Wildlife attack
- Personnel missing
- Any life-threatening situation

**SOS procedure:**
1. Activate SOS on satellite device
2. If able, follow up with satellite phone call
3. Prepare for rescue coordination
4. Document incident details
5. Prepare landing zone if helicopter extraction needed

## 6. Wildlife Safety

### 6.1 Wildlife Risk Assessment

**Pre-deployment assessment:**
- Research wildlife in operating area
- Identify high-risk species (bear, moose, cougar, wolves)
- Determine seasonal activity (denning, calving, mating)
- Review recent sightings and incidents
- Consult with camp manager/local experts

### 6.2 Bear Safety

**Bear country requirements:**

| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|:-----|:--------|:------|
| **Bear spray** | Defense against charging bear | Each crew member carries |
| **Bear bangers/air horn** | Deterrent | Noise to scare bears |
| **Bear-proof containers** | Food storage | All food and scented items |
| **Radio/noise** | Prevention | Alert bears to presence |

**Bear encounter procedures:**

| Situation | Black Bear | Grizzly Bear |
|:----------|:-----------|:-------------|
| **At distance** | Make noise, leave area | Make noise, leave area |
| **Approaching** | Stand ground, make noise | Stand ground, appear large |
| **Charging** | Stand ground, spray at 7m | Stand ground, spray at 7m |
| **Contact (defensive)** | Fight back | Play dead |
| **Contact (predatory)** | Fight back | Fight back |

### 6.3 Other Wildlife Hazards

**Moose:**
- Keep 50m+ distance
- Never approach cows with calves
- Give right of way on trails
- Most dangerous in fall (rut) and spring (calving)

**Wolves/Cougars:**
- Travel in groups
- Make noise while moving
- Do not run if encountered
- Make yourself appear large
- Fight back if attacked

**Ungulates (deer, elk, caribou):**
- RPAS may disturb - maintain distance
- Avoid calving/fawning areas
- Comply with any species-specific restrictions

### 6.4 Wildlife and RPAS Operations

**Pre-flight:**
- Scan area for wildlife before launch
- Do not launch if large mammals within 100m
- Be aware of nesting birds

**During flight:**
- Avoid prolonged hover over wildlife
- Maintain minimum 100m vertical/horizontal from large mammals
- Abort if wildlife distress observed
- Do not pursue or harass wildlife

**Documentation:**
- Report all significant wildlife encounters
- Document any wildlife disturbance incidents
- Note species and behavior for future planning

## 7. Emergency Response When Remote

### 7.1 Emergency Planning

**Pre-deployment requirements:**
- Identify nearest medical facility and travel time
- Establish evacuation routes (primary and alternate)
- Confirm helicopter availability for medical evacuation
- Brief all crew on emergency procedures
- Document emergency contacts

### 7.2 Medical Emergency Response

| Severity | Definition | Response |
|:---------|:-----------|:---------|
| **Minor** | First aid treatable, not urgent | Treat on site, continue operations if able |
| **Moderate** | Requires medical attention, stable | Ground operations, transport when practical |
| **Severe** | Life-threatening, unstable | Activate SOS, request helicopter evacuation |

**Medical kit requirements (remote operations):**
- Comprehensive wilderness first aid kit
- SAM splints
- Tourniquet
- Emergency blankets
- Epinephrine (if trained and prescribed)
- Medications for extended deployment

### 7.3 Fire Emergency

**Fire response:**
1. Alert all personnel
2. Evacuate to designated muster point
3. Do not attempt to fight large fires
4. Activate emergency communication
5. Move to pre-identified safe area
6. Signal for rescue if trapped

**Fire prevention:**
- No open fires during fire bans
- Fire extinguisher at charging station
- Smoking in designated areas only
- Generator away from vegetation
- Spark arrester on generator

### 7.4 Weather Emergency

**Severe weather response:**
- Monitor forecasts continuously
- Establish weather triggers for operations suspension
- Secure all equipment before storms
- Take shelter in robust structures
- Do not attempt evacuation during severe weather

### 7.5 Equipment Emergency

**Aircraft loss/crash:**
1. Mark last known position
2. Do not endanger crew for recovery
3. Plan recovery considering terrain and hazards
4. Document incident thoroughly
5. Report per SMS procedures

## 8. Weather Monitoring

### 8.1 Weather Information Sources

| Source | Information | Update Frequency |
|:-------|:------------|:-----------------|
| **Satellite messenger weather** | Basic forecast | On request |
| **Aviation weather (via satellite)** | TAFs, METARs, AIRMETs | As published |
| **Portable weather station** | Local conditions | Continuous |
| **Visual observation** | Actual conditions | Continuous |
| **Camp weather station** | If available | Varies |

### 8.2 Weather Monitoring Equipment

**Required:**
- Portable anemometer (wind speed/direction)
- Thermometer
- Barometer (or altimeter with pressure setting)

**Recommended:**
- Portable weather station
- Cloud height measurement capability
- Satellite weather receiver

### 8.3 Weather Decision Making

**Weather minimums for remote operations:**

| Condition | Minimum | Action if Below |
|:----------|:--------|:----------------|
| **Visibility** | 3 km | Suspend operations |
| **Ceiling** | 500 ft AGL | Suspend operations |
| **Wind** | As per aircraft limits | Suspend operations |
| **Precipitation** | Light or none | Suspend in moderate+ |
| **Lightning** | >15 km | Suspend operations, seek shelter |

**Remote operations conservative factors:**
- Limited weather data accuracy
- Rapid mountain weather changes
- Reduced escape options
- Longer recovery if issues arise

## 9. Working Alone Considerations

### 9.1 When Working Alone is Permitted

**Working alone ONLY permitted when:**
- Level 1 (Near Remote) site classification
- Reliable communication (cell or satellite)
- Check-in protocol established
- No wildlife high-risk designation
- Weather stable and favorable
- Physical fitness confirmed
- Supervisor approval obtained

**Working alone NEVER permitted:**
- Level 2 or Level 3 sites
- Bear high-activity areas
- Extreme weather conditions
- Operations requiring observer
- Personnel with medical conditions
- First-time site visits

### 9.2 Working Alone Protocols

**Required protocols:**
1. Complete working alone plan
2. Check in before starting work
3. Check in every 2 hours during work
4. Check in when work complete
5. Overdue response triggered after 30 minutes missed check-in

**Working alone plan contents:**
- Exact location (GPS coordinates)
- Planned activities
- Expected duration
- Vehicle description and location
- Check-in schedule
- Emergency contacts
- Return time estimate

### 9.3 Overdue Response

**If worker fails to check in:**

| Time Overdue | Action |
|:-------------|:-------|
| **15 minutes** | Attempt to contact via all methods |
| **30 minutes** | Alert supervisor |
| **1 hour** | Initiate search protocol |
| **2 hours** | Contact emergency services |

## 10. Helicopter/Fixed-Wing Coordination

### 10.1 Aircraft Coordination Requirements

**When sharing airspace with helicopters/fixed-wing:**
- Establish communication before RPAS flight
- Brief pilots on RPAS operations
- Define operating altitudes and areas
- Create landing/takeoff corridors clear of RPAS
- Establish right-of-way (manned aircraft always has priority)

### 10.2 Helicopter Operations Coordination

**Pre-coordination:**

| Item | Requirement |
|:-----|:------------|
| **Helipad location** | Minimum 100m from RPAS operations |
| **Communication** | VHF frequency agreed |
| **Notification** | RPAS ops notified of all helicopter movements |
| **Grounding** | RPAS grounded 10 min before helicopter arrival |
| **Resume** | RPAS ops resume 5 min after helicopter departure |

**During helicopter operations:**
- All RPAS grounded and secured
- Personnel clear of rotor wash
- Secure loose equipment
- Confirm helicopter departed before launch

### 10.3 Fixed-Wing Coordination

**Float plane operations:**
- Operate RPAS away from water approaches
- Ground RPAS when aircraft expected
- Monitor for unscheduled arrivals
- Keep operations below 400 ft AGL near water

### 10.4 Radio Communications

**Standard radio calls:**

| Situation | Call |
|:----------|:-----|
| **Before launch** | "[Camp], [Callsign], launching drone operations [location], [altitude], [duration]" |
| **Helicopter arriving** | "[Callsign], [Camp], helicopter inbound 10 minutes, confirm drone grounded" |
| **Operations complete** | "[Camp], [Callsign], drone operations complete, landing zone clear" |

### 10.5 Emergency Aircraft Coordination

**If emergency aircraft requires access:**
1. Immediately ground all RPAS
2. Clear helipads and landing zones
3. Assist as directed
4. Do not resume until authorized
